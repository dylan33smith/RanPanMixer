The two tests agree only after the extra draws. On a single draw per cell they disagreed — sign test p = 0.174, paired z -3.26 — because PanMixer's draw noise was flipping marginal individuals' signs. Five draws per cell settled it. The median difference barely moved; the dispersion fell.
Our column is an exact expectation; theirs is a 5-draw mean. A paired test treats their value as fixed. Residual noise on their side is about 29% of the effect, down from 66% on one draw.

They offer a frontier. We offer a point.

PanMixer's capacity knob moves its alteration rate across more than fifteen percentage points. Our whole released privacy range moves ours by +0.0935 points.

Our level is reached at acc_ceiling 0.50, which is η = 0 — no protection applied, zero target information. So the comparison above is between two release forms, not between two privacy knobs. It is not ours to quote as a privacy result.

Every individual, paired

For 11 of 44, PanMixer is more faithful at every setting it offers. That is a quarter of the cohort, and no matched-fidelity point exists for them. The same 11 individuals are exactly those where the paired test says we lose — two independent routes to the same split.

Our grid, and why η is per individual

acc_ceiling is a Pinsker bound. The true ceiling is closer to 0.5 than every label here. And it is defined against the extremal decoy pair — a measured accuracy against any other decoy must be compared to that pair's own ceiling, which is roughly 0.12 lower.

What af_loss is actually measuring

Their code treats an uncalled cell as just another allele, so a cell whose called/uncalled status flips scores exactly like a real allele swap. What that penalises is divergence from the row being replaced — and the two mechanisms differ there by construction, not by accident.

Our mechanism emits a fixed-support path — nearly the same uncalled set for every individual. PanMixer edits a copy of that individual's own row, so its uncalled set tracks the original almost perfectly. Genuine allele swaps, where both cells are called, are within 1% of each other (111,615 against 112,922).

The verdict runs from “they win on all 44 at p = 1e−13” to “we win 31 of 44” — with nothing about either mechanism changing. Only the bookkeeping for cells the two tools are not really disagreeing about. So af_loss as shipped cannot settle this comparison. The shipped figure is still the one to publish, because it is what a reader of their paper reproduces — but it has to be published as policy-dependent, and “they distort allele frequencies less for every one of the 44” is a statement about the bookkeeping, not about the mechanisms.

And the LD advantage is 1.30×, not 7.5×

In their LD code, p_A and p_Bs are averaged over the cells that are present, while the joint term is averaged over all 88 — a missing cell silently counts as a non-observation rather than being excluded. The two halves of the same quantity use different denominators, which inflates the result wherever cells are missing.

Under the shipped denominator, the largest 6 pairs out of about 7,500 carry 94% of their total and 60% of ours — against 16% and 10% under a consistent one. So the 7.5× figure is set by a handful of pairs with missing cells; it is not a mean over 7,462 pairs in any useful sense. We do disturb local linkage less, on all 44 individuals, and the honest size of it is 1.30×. The smaller number is the more robust one.

This reproduces what the earlier cross-build run found, now that the build confound is gone.
Both evaluators were reimplemented from their definitions and matched exactly. No PanMixer code imported: af_loss agreed to 0.000e+00 on our cell and 8.674e-19 on theirs; ld_loss agreed to 0.000e+00 on numerator, denominator and ratio. The pipeline is checked, not just its output.
What this still cannot say. capacity is a utility-loss budget and bounds nothing about distinguishability; acc_ceiling does not transfer to their mechanism. So every row above compares the two tools at each one's own endpoint, not at matched privacy. Putting both on one build removed the build confound and left that one untouched. Also: ld_loss's window is 5 position units despite being named for 5 kb, and its denominator is counted per row, so it moves with the release.

What has not been run

  • The attacks. A1 (the optimal distinguisher) and A3 are specified and the instrument verifies exactly on a real Arm 1 release, but no powered run exists. The driver is not written.
  • PanMixer's privacy evaluators — gap score, diploid gap score, MIA — on the new releases. Previously run on the old grid, where they were defeated by our η = 0 release too, which makes them a statement about release form rather than about the tilt.
  • Read mapping on the new grid. Done once on the old grid: no harm, at the same order as PanMixer's own degradation.
