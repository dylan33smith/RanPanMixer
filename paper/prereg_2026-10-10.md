# Pre-registration — RanPanMixer, Phase B, both arms

**Status: ⚠ DRAFT, UNSIGNED.** Fields marked **[OPEN]** are not decided and this file
is not in force until every one of them is filled and the file is committed as
`SIGNED`. Standing constraint 6 freezes everything here before any attack outcome is
inspected, so a field filled with a guess is worse than a field left open.

**Scope.** One file, both arms. Arm 1 is the PanMixer head-to-head on the 44 HPRC
cohort members; Arm 2 is the external target. Shared fields are stated once.

**What this document is for.** Not administrative. Everything below can be chosen in a
way that makes a result look better, and once an attack outcome has been seen there is
no way to prove a choice was not made that way. The point is to make that
impossible rather than to promise it did not happen.

---

## 0. The framing this pre-registration rests on (settled 2026-10-10)

**The headline privacy claim rests on the proof plus the chr21/genome-scale
measurements. The two-chromosome toy is the CORRECTNESS DEMONSTRATION, not the
claim.** The toy shows the mechanism is implemented correctly and calibrates what
"the budget is diluted at genome scale" means — it is 9 chain positions and is not a
prediction about a 25-million-position genome.

⚠ **Consequence, and it removes a field this file would otherwise need.** The
contiguous-window secondary utility arm is **DROPPED**. Its only job was to show the
tilt is visible somewhere; `logs/B_toy_genome_demo.md` now does that without costing
a second release of the same person — which would have composed and degraded the
guarantee (standing constraint 5). One release per genome, one utility function.

## 1. The privacy parameter

| field | value |
|---|---|
| `tau` grid | **[OPEN]** — recommendation below |
| calibration | `eta_tau = arctanh(tau)`, `epsilon_tau = 2 * eta_tau`, `p_succ_bound = (1 + tau)/2`. Fixed by `src/mechanism/calibration.py` and cross-checked against the glossary table by a test. |

**[OPEN] Recommendation: pin all seven of 0, 0.05, 0.10, 0.25, 0.50, 0.90, 0.99**, and
declare what each point is FOR, because a grid whose points carry different claims
must say so:

- **`tau` = 0 — the floor, and mandatory.** An untilted draw from `baseline_model`,
  carrying zero target information. It is the control that stops a cohort-level
  utility figure reading as a free lunch: on chr21 a `tau` = 0 release already matches
  the target at 85.19% of its called cells purely because two humans share most
  alleles.
- **`tau` = 0.05 to 0.25 — the defensible operating range.** `tau` = 0.10 bounds
  equal-prior identification at 0.55. These are the only points we claim as operating
  settings.
- **`tau` = 0.50 — context**, reported, not claimed.
- **`tau` = 0.90 and 0.99 — bound-checking only.** A bound must be checked where it is
  loose. These are past any setting we would propose and must be labelled so in every
  table.

Rationale for seven rather than matching PanMixer's 50 capacities: their grid is 50
nested subsets of ONE presampled draw (verified at `stacker.py:57`), so its density is
not evidence. Ours is seven independent operating points.

## 2. The utility function

| field | value |
|---|---|
| `phi_v` | an indicator: 1 where the released allele equals the target's at `v`, else 0. A position where the target has no call scores 0 for every donor — **not** a wildcard match. |
| `w_v` | **[OPEN]** — recommendation below (deferred decision R3) |
| `beta_v` scope, Arm 1 | `chain`. **Settled, and not a fork:** `readable` is produced only by the external-target pipeline, and a readable mask derived from `G` is all-True because the chain axis IS `G`'s own deconstructed site list. |
| `beta_v` scope, Arm 2 | **[OPEN]** — recommendation below (deferred decisions R1, R4) |
| normalisation | sums to 1 over the **22 autosomes together**, each chromosome taking a share (standing constraint 4, decision B). |
| share allocation | `T_c / T_total` by **variant count** (decision J), with `T_c` on the FULL 88-haplotype cohort, fixed before any target is selected. ⚠ The shares are pinned as a RULE, not as 22 numbers: `T_c` is an output of preprocessing. |
| diploid aggregation | the MEAN over haplotypes, so each chain runs at `eta_tau / ploidy`. Asserted; a sum is the error this project has already made once. |
| bound | `u` in [0,1] globally, asserted at runtime on the COMPOSITE, not per factor. |

**[OPEN] Arm 2 `beta_v` scope — recommendation: `chain`, and do NOT take R1's fix.**
R1 observes that `beta_v` is not normalised over readable positions, so ~18.5% of the
budget is inert and `u` cannot exceed ~0.815. Its fix is to normalise over
`in_chain & readable`. **R4 kills that fix:** `readable` is computed from the target
VCF's own site list and is identical across our ten test targets ONLY because they
were cut from one panel. A genuinely novel genome arrives with its own site list, so
`readable` would differ per target and normalising over it would be target-dependent
— the exact standing-constraint-1 violation R1 exists to avoid. Taking a fix that
works in the harness and fails in deployment is worse than carrying the known cost.
So: `chain`, with the inert share reported per target as a stated ceiling. This also
keeps ONE utility function across both arms, which constraint 8 requires for the
head-to-head.

**[OPEN] `w_v` — recommendation: keep `1/support(v)`, declared as INHERITED rather
than argued.** It came from PanMixer, where it was a cost weight doing a different
job, and it upweights poorly-called rather than informative positions. But on this
cohort it is nearly uniform: median support 88, **98.6% of chain positions at support
>= 80 holding 96.8% of the weight**, and the top 1% of positions by weight holding
2.8%. So the 88x ratio exists in principle and applies to almost nothing. Changing it
now would invalidate every recorded number for a difference measured at under 4% of
the weight. The honest move is to keep it and say it is inherited.

## 3. The primary endpoint

| field | value |
|---|---|
| primary | **`E[differ]`, the expected number of released cells differing from the target, computed EXACTLY from the posterior marginals.** Reported against its own arm's ceiling, never against 1. |
| route | `ffbs.state_marginals` then `ffbs.expected_mismatches`. A linear functional of the marginals: no sampling, no differencing. |
| ⚠ forbidden route | **The finite-difference derivative of `log Z_p` must NOT be used at genome-wide scope.** Measured 2026-10-10: it returns a **negative** `Var_Q(u)` at chr21's genetic-length share and scatters over a 5x range at 1/22, because its second difference is a cancellation against ~1e-13 of absolute error while `Var` shrinks with the square of the share. It survives only as a chr21-scale diagnostic and anything quoting it must say so. |
| secondary, reported | `two_target_indistinguishability`, `ratio_spread`, `log_z_p`, `donor_switches`, `u_ceiling`, and the released-path churn. |
| ⚠ not adopted | `af_loss`, `ld_loss`. In Arm 1 they ARE defined — the release set is cohort-shaped — so this is a **scope choice**, not an impossibility, and `docs/terms.md` says so. Arm 1 adopts `utility_loss_panmixer` only (decision H). |

## 4. The attacker set

Every attack carries a POSITIVE CONTROL: a stated condition under which it must
demonstrably SUCCEED. ⚠ This is mandatory, not optional. The repository's own forecast
is a chance-level null at the operating configuration (R5: signal-to-noise about
1:900, and constraint 5 permits one release per genome). **A null without a power
statement is not evidence (standing constraint 7); a null with one is the privacy
evidence.** `src/attacks.py` makes `positive_control` a required field so this cannot
be skipped.

| | attack | arm | statistic | chance | positive control |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | exact likelihood-ratio distinguisher over matched (target, decoy) pairs | both | `eta_tau [u(p,y) - u(q,y)] - [log Z_p - log Z_q]` | 0.5 | (a) a mechanism miscalibrated with `epsilon_tau` for `eta_tau` must MOVE its advantage, measured against the correct mechanism's own value; (b) on the toy it must ATTAIN `(1 + TV)/2` exactly |
| **A2** | exact enumeration of TV and `ratio_spread` over all admissible input pairs | both | — | — | already passes: the constructed pair saturates `exp(epsilon_tau)` exactly, and the doubled exponent fails it at every `tau` |
| **A3** | rarity-weighted genotype linkage, re-posed as 1-of-2 | both | PanMixer's score, with `ref_af = 1 - alt_af` fixed | 0.5 | must link the UNOBFUSCATED target at near-100%, and must sit at chance against a `tau` = 0 release |
| **A4** | segment-restricted likelihood ratio over one haplotype block | both | A1 restricted to a block, multiplicity-corrected | 0.5 | must find a planted block copied verbatim from the target |

⚠⚠ **`p_succ_bound` IS NOT A DETECTION THRESHOLD.** Measured 2026-10-10: a doubled
tilt exponent raises the optimal attacker only to **0.8412 against a bound of
0.9500** at `tau` = 0.90, and stays inside the bound at every `tau`, because the worst
exact TV on the toy reaches only 27.4-43.1% of `tau`. Any control phrased as "the
attacker must exceed `p_succ_bound`" is unfalsifiable. Controls are paired comparisons
or saturating constructions.

⚠⚠ **TWO ATTACKS ARE ILL POSED IN ARM 2 AND MUST NOT BE RUN THERE.** Enforced by
`src/attacks.py`, which raises rather than returning a number:
- **Database search** has no correct answer — the target is outside `G`, `D`, every
  panel and the attack database by construction, so a 0% link rate is the
  experimental design and cannot come out any other way.
- **Membership inference inverts** — there is no positive class, and the release is a
  mosaic of cohort donors, so a high match fraction identifies **donors**, not the
  target. Read as membership it would reverse the conclusion.

**[OPEN] The decoy construction**, which is as load-bearing as the attacker:
- the extremal pair (differing on both haplotypes everywhere) — **settled, mandatory**
- plus a seeded random sample: **[OPEN] sample size and seed**
- **[OPEN] the relatedness rule.** The same pedigree rule that produced the eligible
  target pool must govern decoy sampling, and it is written nowhere. A relative is the
  hardest decoy, so excluding relatives weakens the attacker in the direction that
  flatters us.

**[OPEN] The minimum detectable advantage the experiment is powered for.** Without it
a null is uninterpretable. This is the single most important open field in this file.

## 7. What is deliberately NOT claimed

Stating these here, before any outcome, is what stops them being discovered as
limitations later.

1. **The guarantee covers the released path only** — not the raw target genome, not
   the graph, not any other released statistic about the same person.
2. **Arm 1's guarantee is conditional on `G`**, which was built from all 44 assemblies
   including the target's. 126,065 chr21 alt alleles are cohort singletons, so their
   presence as a record in `G` is attributable to one donor. That is leakage from
   publishing `G`, which Arm 1's premise accepts and which PanMixer shares.
3. **Arm 1's 44 releases are jointly correlated and the JOINT distribution is not
   `tau`-indistinguishable.** Each release's donor panel is other people's true
   haplotypes. The per-release bound holds; the joint bound does not.
4. **The two arms' ceilings differ** (~97.3% vs ~90% of non-ref alleles), so fidelity
   is comparable within an arm across mechanisms and never across arms.
5. **No claim whatsoever about protecting donors in Arm 2.** `tau` bounds target
   indistinguishability and nothing else.
6. **3.0% of autosomal sequence has no genetic distance** and is released under a
   declared rate; **3.27% more** sits in interior zero-cM plateaus and is emitted as
   verbatim donor runs.
7. **The N-gap policy's consequence**, now that gaps are skipped: nothing is
   manufactured, but the 17.4 Mb of real map-silent sequence still carries a declared
   rather than a measured rate.
