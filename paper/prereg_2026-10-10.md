### Arm 2's `beta_v` scope is `chain`, and R1's fix is DECLINED on R4's grounds

R1 observes that `beta_v` is not normalised over readable positions, so ~18.5% of the
budget is inert and `u` cannot exceed ~0.815. Its fix is to normalise over
`in_chain & readable`. **R4 kills that fix:** `readable` is computed from the target
VCF's own site list and is identical across our ten test targets ONLY because they
were cut from one panel. A genuinely novel genome arrives with its own site list, so
`readable` would differ per target and normalising over it would be target-dependent
— the exact standing-constraint-1 violation R1 exists to avoid. Taking a fix that
works in the harness and fails in deployment is worse than carrying the known cost.
So: `chain`, with the inert share reported per target as a stated ceiling. This also
keeps ONE utility function across both arms, which constraint 8 requires for the
head-to-head.

### `w_v` stays `1 / support(v)`, declared as inherited
 It came from PanMixer, where it was a cost weight doing a different
job, and it upweights poorly-called rather than informative positions. But on this
cohort it is nearly uniform: median support 88, **98.6% of chain positions at support
>= 80 holding 96.8% of the weight**, and the top 1% of positions by weight holding
2.8%. So the 88x ratio exists in principle and applies to almost nothing. Changing it
now would invalidate every recorded number for a difference measured at under 4% of
the weight. The honest move is to keep it and say it is inherited.

## 4. The attacker set

Every attack carries a POSITIVE CONTROL: a stated condition under which it must
demonstrably SUCCEED. ⚠ This is mandatory, not optional. The repository's own forecast
is a chance-level null at the operating configuration (R5: signal-to-noise about
1:900, and constraint 5 permits one release per genome). **A null without a power
statement is not evidence (standing constraint 7); a null with one is the privacy
evidence.** `src/attacks.py` makes `positive_control` a required field so this cannot
be skipped.

| | attack | arm | statistic | chance | positive control |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | exact likelihood-ratio distinguisher over matched (target, decoy) pairs | both | `eta_tau [u(p,y) - u(q,y)] - [log Z_p - log Z_q]` | 0.5 | (a) a mechanism miscalibrated with `epsilon_tau` for `eta_tau` must MOVE its advantage, measured against the correct mechanism's own value; (b) on the toy it must ATTAIN `(1 + TV)/2` exactly |
| **A2** | exact enumeration of TV and `ratio_spread` over all admissible input pairs | both | — | — | already passes: the constructed pair saturates `exp(epsilon_tau)` exactly, and the doubled exponent fails it at every `tau` |
| **A3** | rarity-weighted genotype linkage, re-posed as 1-of-2 | both | PanMixer's score, with `ref_af = 1 - alt_af` fixed | 0.5 | must link the UNOBFUSCATED target at near-100%, and must sit at chance against a `tau` = 0 release |
| **A4** | segment-restricted likelihood ratio over one haplotype block | both | A1 restricted to a block, multiplicity-corrected | 0.5 | must find a planted block copied verbatim from the target |

⚠⚠ **`p_succ_bound` IS NOT A DETECTION THRESHOLD.** Measured 2026-10-10: a doubled
tilt exponent raises the optimal attacker only to **0.8412 against a bound of
0.9500** at `tau` = 0.90, and stays inside the bound at every `tau`, because the worst
exact TV on the toy reaches only 27.4-43.1% of `tau`. Any control phrased as "the
attacker must exceed `p_succ_bound`" is unfalsifiable. Controls are paired comparisons
or saturating constructions.

⚠⚠ **TWO ATTACKS ARE ILL POSED IN ARM 2 AND MUST NOT BE RUN THERE.** Enforced by
`src/attacks.py`, which raises rather than returning a number:
- **Database search** has no correct answer — the target is outside `G`, `D`, every
  panel and the attack database by construction, so a 0% link rate is the
  experimental design and cannot come out any other way.
- **Membership inference inverts** — there is no positive class, and the release is a
  mosaic of cohort donors, so a high match fraction identifies **donors**, not the
  target. Read as membership it would reverse the conclusion.

### The decoy construction (settled 2026-10-10) — THREE conditions, reported separately

A decoy's difficulty is as load-bearing as the attacker's optimality: an easy decoy
produces a null that says nothing. So three conditions run, and the SPREAD BETWEEN
THEM is itself a reported result.

| condition | decoy | why |
|---|---|---|
| **D1 extremal** | the constructed pair differing on both haplotypes everywhere | saturates the ratio bound exactly; the sharp, worst-case case |
| **D2 nearest** | the panel sample with the highest genotype concordance to the target, over the chain's site axis | the HARDEST REAL decoy. Deterministic, so no seed |
| **D3 random** | one sample drawn uniformly from the eligible pool per release | the easy baseline, and what an unlucky adversary actually faces |

⚠ **D2 REPLACES A "RELATED DECOY" CONDITION, WHICH IS NOT RUNNABLE AND THE REASON
MATTERS.** The intuition is right — a first-degree relative shares about half the
genome and is therefore the hardest decoy — but **all ten test targets were selected
to have NO first-degree relative anywhere in the 3,202-sample panel.** That is the
fifth eligibility rule of 2026-09-29: 1,686 of the 3,085 eligible samples have such a
relative, so the strict pool is 1,399 and the targets were drawn from it precisely to
stop the attack matching a relative instead of the target. Asking for a related decoy
now would contradict the rule that built the target set.
**D2 is the better instrument anyway**: the point of a related decoy is that it is
genetically close, and nearest-by-concordance IS the closest available sample, whether
or not a pedigree records the link. It needs no pedigree file (none is on disk) and it
is the actual worst case rather than a proxy for it.
⚠ If a pedigree-literal comparison is wanted later, it needs a SECOND target set drawn
from the 1,686 excluded samples, and that set is not interchangeable with this one.

### [OPEN] The design must draw from BOTH hypotheses (raised 2026-10-10)

⚠⚠ **THE DESIGN ABOVE CANNOT MEASURE THE QUANTITY IT IS COMPARED TO.** `p_succ_bound`
= (1 + `tau`)/2 bounds the EQUAL-PRIOR success probability

    0.5 * P_p(Lambda > 0) + 0.5 * P_q(Lambda < 0),

which requires draws from BOTH input paths. The design above draws every release from
`Q_p` only (the "reuse" row says so explicitly), so it yields the ONE-SIDED rate
`P_p(Lambda > 0)` — and **that quantity is not bounded by (1 + `tau`)/2.** From

    balanced = 0.5*TPR + 0.5*(1 - FPR) <= (1 + TV)/2   =>   TPR <= TV + FPR,

an attacker fully respecting the theorem, with FPR 0.5, reaches TPR = `tau` + 0.5,
which EXCEEDS (1 + `tau`)/2 at every `tau` > 0. And a degenerate attacker that always
answers "p" scores **1.0** one-sided while sitting at exactly chance when balanced.
**So the comparison would have reported a privacy violation with the theorem intact** —
`scripts/arm2_attack.py` performed exactly that comparison until 2026-10-10.

The fix is one extra release per (trial, condition), drawn from `Q_q`, with `Lambda`
evaluated by the same statistic and the same normalisers. That changes the cost, and
the balanced estimator is MORE precise per observation — `delta` = 0.879/sqrt(n)
against the one-sided 1.243/sqrt(n) — so matching the committed `delta` needs fewer
draws, not more:

| design | draws/target per `tau` | `delta` | releases | hours |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| committed, one-sided | 50 | 0.0556 | 3,500 | 9.1 | ⚠ **INVALID — not comparable to the bound** |
| balanced, `delta` matched | **25** | 0.0556 | 7,000 | **18.3** |
| balanced, same draws | 50 | 0.0393 | 14,000 | 36.6 |
| balanced, cost matched | 12 | 0.0803 | 3,360 | 8.8 |

⚠ **THIS FIELD IS OPEN AND THE FILE IS THEREFORE NOT SIGNABLE.** Dylan chooses. The
recommendation is **balanced at 25 draws**: it preserves the pre-registered `delta` =
0.056 exactly, so every claim already written in this document about what the
experiment licenses (`tau` >= 0.25) stands unchanged, and it doubles rather than
quadruples the cost. The cost-matched 12-draw option would move `delta` to 0.080,
which no longer resolves below the bound at `tau` = 0.25 (permitted 0.625 against a
statement of < 0.580) and would silently narrow the claim.

⚠ Until this is settled, `scripts/arm2_attack.py` defaults to the BALANCED design and
its `--one-sided` mode refuses to compare anything to `p_succ_bound`, printing why
instead of a number.

---

⚠⚠ **THE EXPERIMENT AND THE PROOF COVER DIFFERENT PARTS OF THE GRID, AND SAYING SO IS
THE POINT.** An empirical attack's value is the GAP between what the bound permits and
what is measured. That gap is large where the bound is loose and vanishes where it is
tight, so a single `delta` cannot be informative everywhere:

| `tau` | the bound permits | the experiment can say | it adds |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0.05 | 0.525 | < 0.556 | **nothing — the bound is tighter** |
| 0.10 | 0.550 | < 0.556 | **nothing** |
| 0.25 | 0.625 | < 0.556 | **+0.069** |
| 0.50 | 0.750 | < 0.556 | **+0.194** |
| 0.90 | 0.950 | < 0.556 | **+0.394** |
| 0.99 | 0.995 | < 0.556 | **+0.439** |

**So the PROOF carries `tau` <= 0.10 and the EXPERIMENT carries `tau` >= 0.25.** At
`tau` = 0.05 the bound is 0.525, within 2.5 points of a coin, and no affordable
experiment resolves below it: reaching `delta` = 0.023 would cost 21,000 releases
(54.8 hours) to confirm 0.525 with 0.523. This is a general property of a formally
private mechanism rather than a shortcoming of ours, and reporting it that way is more
honest than running an underpowered attack at every point and calling the uniform null
a result.
⚠ **At `tau` = 0.05 and 0.10 the measured accuracy is still REPORTED, with its
`delta`, and labelled as not resolving below the bound.** An omitted row is forbidden
by the reporting contract; a row that overstates what it shows is worse.

**Why not cheaper still.** 25 draws (`delta` = 0.079, 4.6 hours) is also informative at
`tau` >= 0.25, but only just — it would state "< 0.579" against a permitted 0.625, a
gap of 0.046 against 50 draws' 0.069. Doubling to 50 buys a 50% tighter statement at
the one `tau` where the margin is thin, for 4.5 extra hours.

⚠ **AND A COST ERROR WORTH RECORDING.** An earlier version of this section specified
300 draws and claimed 3,000 trials for 7.8 hours. Trials are PER `tau`, and there are
seven, so that design is 21,000 releases and **54.8 hours** — seven times the stated
cost, for a `delta` targeting the one `tau` where the experiment cannot contribute.

⚠⚠ **MULTIPLE DRAWS PER TARGET ARE AN EVALUATION DEVICE AND NOT A RELEASE
PROCEDURE.** Standing constraint 5 permits ONE release per genome, because independent
re-releases of the same path compose and degrade the guarantee. These are TEST targets
— held-out panel samples, not people being protected — and 50 draws of one test
target is an experiment measuring a distribution, not 50 releases of a person. **No
deployed release may ever be drawn more than once**, and nothing in this design is
evidence that it may.

**Why the power statement is the load-bearing field.** The repository forecasts a
chance-level null at the operating configuration (R5: signal-to-noise about 1:900).
A measured accuracy of 0.5 is equally consistent with "the mechanism is private" and
"the experiment was too small to see the leak". Only a stated `delta` separates them,
and it cannot be chosen after the result is seen. For reference, what each `n` buys:

| trials | detectable advantage | claims at |
|---|---|---|
| 10 | 0.393 | nothing |
| 100 | 0.124 | `tau` = 0.25 |
| 619 | 0.050 | `tau` = 0.10 |
| **3,000** | **0.023** | **`tau` = 0.05** |

| field | value |
|---|---|
| source | 1000G 30x GRCh38, 3,202 samples. ⚠ NOT 1000G Phase 3 (GRCh37) — the build mismatch measured at 0.30% vs 75.88% record agreement. |
| Arm 1 "self" | the target's **own assembly-derived path**, defined for all 44. **Not** its 1000G panel row, which exists for only 39 of the 44 (decision I). PanMixer's panel-row semantics run as a labelled parity column. |
| Arm 2 | cohort overlap removed before loading: the 39 HPRC members present in the panel and their 78 first-degree relatives. |
| Arm 1 membership | ✅ **TWO VARIANTS, both reported** (settled 2026-10-10). **V1 CONSERVATIVE, and it carries the claim:** the panel minus ALL HPRC members and minus their 78 first-degree relatives. **V2 PERMISSIVE:** the full panel with members and relatives retained. One fixed database per variant for all 44 targets, so the attack is literally the same test for every target. |

**Why two variants, and why the difference between them is a result.** Our Arm 1
release for target X is a mosaic of the OTHER 43 members' haplotypes, so those members'
own genotypes score WELL against it — the release is built from their sequence. If they
sit in the attack database they act as near-perfect decoys, the argmax lands on a
cohort member rather than on X, and the measured linkage rate falls. **Privacy looks
better, and it is an artifact of database composition rather than of the mechanism.**
X's own parents are the sharpest case: they share about half X's genome.

So V2 is the flattering direction and V1 is the conservative one. **Reporting both
quantifies how much of the apparent privacy is an artifact** — a number nobody has,
and one that applies to PanMixer's published result as much as to ours, since its
database retains the overlap. ⚠ V1 carries the claim; V2 is reported beside it and is
never quoted alone.

## 7. What is deliberately NOT claimed

Stating these here, before any outcome, is what stops them being discovered as
limitations later.

1. **The guarantee covers the released path only** — not the raw target genome, not
   the graph, not any other released statistic about the same person.
2. **Arm 1's guarantee is conditional on `G`**, which was built from all 44 assemblies
   including the target's. 126,065 chr21 alt alleles are cohort singletons, so their
   presence as a record in `G` is attributable to one donor. That is leakage from
   publishing `G`, which Arm 1's premise accepts and which PanMixer shares.
3. **Arm 1's 44 releases are jointly correlated and the JOINT distribution is not
   `tau`-indistinguishable.** Each release's donor panel is other people's true
   haplotypes. The per-release bound holds; the joint bound does not.
4. **The two arms' ceilings differ** (~97.3% vs ~90% of non-ref alleles), so fidelity
   is comparable within an arm across mechanisms and never across arms.
5. **No claim whatsoever about protecting donors in Arm 2.** `tau` bounds target
   indistinguishability and nothing else.
6. **3.0% of autosomal sequence has no genetic distance** and is released under a
   declared rate; **3.27% more** sits in interior zero-cM plateaus and is emitted as
   verbatim donor runs.
7. **The N-gap policy's consequence**, now that gaps are skipped: nothing is
   manufactured, but the 17.4 Mb of real map-silent sequence still carries a declared
   rather than a measured rate.
