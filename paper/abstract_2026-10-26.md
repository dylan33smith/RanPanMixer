## The abstract

Pangenome graphs are built from the haplotypes of real people, and the tools that
sanitize them — allele-editing under a utility budget, differentially private graph
release — address the disclosure risk carried by the cohort that builds the reference.
A different problem is unaddressed: releasing a path for a target who is not in the
graph, with a stated bound on how well any attacker can tell that release apart from
one generated for someone else.

We present a randomized path release mechanism for that setting. A Li-Stephens copying
model over the public cohort's haplotypes defines a baseline distribution on
cohort-supported paths; the release exponentially tilts that baseline toward paths
agreeing with the target, with the tilt calibrated against a utility bounded in [0,1].
The support and the baseline are functions of the graph and the cohort alone, so the
tilt is the only target-dependent term, and any two input paths induce release
distributions at total variation distance at most `tau`. An attacker holding the graph,
the mechanism, the target and a decoy therefore cannot say which of the two a release
came from with accuracy above (1 + `tau`)/2. For a given cohort and target we compute
that ceiling exactly rather than through the calibrated worst case: from a KL divergence
evaluated over all 22 autosomes (T = 22,134,562 positions, no sampling) against an
allele-flipped decoy, which places the release on a ceiling that runs from 0.500 to
1.000 across the knob's full range.

The guarantee is the upper bound; we also measure from below. Five implemented
distinguishers — optimal, nearest-decoy, random-decoy, rarity-weighted genotype linkage
and a segment-restricted likelihood ratio, all with passing positive controls — stay
under the exact ceiling at every point of a privacy-matched grid, the strongest reaching
balanced accuracy 0.826 where the ceiling permits 1.000 and 0.660 where it permits 0.756.
The gap between what the mechanism permits and what any attack we built achieves is the
budget it leaves unspent.

What that budget buys is small, and measuring it is this paper's main empirical result.
Moving the ceiling from 0.500 to 0.756 recovers 1,520 additional non-reference alleles of
2,357,272 (+0.064%), and the full range to 1.000 recovers 3,041 (+0.13%) — 0.156% of the
recoverable gap in expected utility. The release nonetheless does no harm to the graph's
downstream use: substituting one obfuscated member into a 44-haplotype reference changes
about 20% of that member's allele cells while read-mapping rates for five external donors
move by at most 0.063 percentage points, the same order as the 0.01 points reported for
prior work. Against the published attacks of a prior cohort-obfuscation tool, our
genome-wide releases of all 44 cohort members are linked for 0 of 44 targets at every
setting but the largest, where the ceiling is already vacuous and 1 of 44 is linked,
against 55 of 100 for that tool's own releases; the untilted release is linked 0 of 44
as well, so the result is a property of the released object — a cohort mosaic — and not
of the tilt. The prior tool's capacity parameter stops altering its output past half its
range, having exhausted its candidate edits, while our ceiling is monotone and live
throughout.

## What I changed from your half-draft, and why

1. **"our releases link 0/44 targets at every setting" is not what the table says.**
   `pm_attacks.md` reads 0/44 for the Gap Score at `eta_tau` 0, 250, 500, 1262 and 2821
   and **1/44 at 5642**; membership inference puts **1/44 at rank 1 at `eta_tau` = 500**.
   The abstract now says "every setting but the largest" and names that the ceiling is
   already vacuous there. The sentence as drafted would have been refutable from our own
   emitted table.
2. **The 0/44 must not be attributed to the tilt.** The untilted release (`eta_tau` = 0)
   is also linked 0/44, so what defeats their linkage attacks is the release FORM — a
   cohort mosaic — not the privacy knob. This is the "two nulls prove less than they look
   like" point from 2026-10-15, and an abstract that implies otherwise is the most
   quotable overclaim available in this paper.
3. **"a proven privacy bound for the maximum accuracy of any two-way distinguisher"**
   splits into two statements that are doing different work, and the draft merged them.
   The *theorem* gives (1 + `tau`)/2, which is ~1 everywhere in the reported regime and
   carries no information. The *number we report* is `acc_ceiling`, computed per
   cohort-and-target from an exact KL. Both are in the abstract, in that order, so a
   reader cannot mistake the second for the first.
4. **`acc_ceiling` is not "the total variation".** It is `(1 + sqrt(kl_exact/2))/2` —
   Pinsker's **upper bound** on the TV, from an exact KL. The true TV is smaller, so the
   true ceiling is closer to 0.5 and the looseness runs in the direction that flatters
   us (bounded at `sqrt(pi/2)` = 1.25 here, by the CLT argument of 2026-10-24). I wrote
   "compute that ceiling exactly ... from a KL divergence", which is true, rather than
   "the total variation", which is not.
5. **"the balanced accuracy of attacks is bounded by the accuracy ceiling" inverts what
   an attack can show.** Standing constraint 4: a failed attacker is a LOWER bound on
   distinguishability. The attacks cannot confirm the bound; they can only fail to reach
   it. Phrased as "we also measure from below" and "any attack we built".
6. **Dropped "with a proven privacy guarantee" as a novelty claim.** privvg / `odgi priv`
   applied the exponential mechanism to a pangenome graph in 2022 and must be cited in
   the intro. The novelty I kept is the setting — a target outside the graph — plus the
   exactly-computed ceiling, neither of which privvg or PanMixer claims.
7. **Utility is a MOVEMENT, never a level.** `e_q_u_over_ceiling` = 0.859 is reached at
   `eta_tau` = 0 by an untilted mosaic carrying zero target information, so "we retain
   86% of utility" is the free-lunch error. Only the +0.064% / +0.13% movements appear.
8. **Named the arms.** The ceiling and the attacks are Arm 2; the read mapping and the
   head-to-head are Arm 1. Your draft's "our releases" spanned both.
9. **Dropped the `tau`-range finding from the abstract.** That `tau`'s whole range buys
   about four cells in 7.6M is a real finding, but it is a finding about a parameterisation
   we no longer use as the knob, and it costs two sentences to state without confusing a
   reader. It belongs in the results.

## Open choices for you

1. **Is the headline the guarantee or the unspent budget?** I wrote it as "here is a
   mechanism with a computable ceiling, and holding the ceiling is cheap because the
   mechanism's reach at genome scale is small". The alternative is to lead with the gap
   itself — "a genome-scale release can be made provably hard to distinguish, and the
   measured cost of doing so is 0.064% of the recoverable non-reference alleles" — which
   is the same facts with the negative result in front. Your call; it changes the first
   sentence and nothing else.
2. **"worst-case decoy" — I did not write it.** D1_extremal is the allele-flipped copy of
   the target, maximally dissimilar, and it is the hardest decoy we evaluated; I found no
   proof in `docs/` that it maximises KL over all admissible decoys. Claiming "worst
   case" needs that proof or a decoy sweep. Until then "an allele-flipped decoy" is what
   the number supports.
3. **Can we compute `acc_ceiling` for PanMixer's releases?** You asked this twice. I do
   not think so, and the reason is structural rather than a missing implementation: their
   mechanism is a knapsack over a fixed candidate list with a seed fixing each block's
   resampled value, so a release is a deterministic function of (`capacity`, seed) and
   there is no release *distribution* over which a KL between two inputs is defined. A
   direct comparison on our metric would require re-posing their optimiser as a sampler,
   which is their mechanism's redesign, not an evaluation. What IS comparable, and what
   the abstract uses, is their own published attack outcome and the saturation visible in
   their own logs. ⚠ Worth an independent check before it reaches the paper as a claim —
   this is my reading of their code, not a measurement.
4. **`epsilon_tau` is not in the abstract.** At the reported grid it is 500 to 11,280
   per release, which reads alarmingly until divided by T (2.39e-07 nats per position,
   3.2e04 to 3.2e07 times more conservative than the published genomic band). A number
   that needs a paragraph to stop being misleading does not belong in an abstract.
5. **Caveats that must reach the paper but not the abstract**: read mapping is chr21,
   one target substituted into 44, full-graph construction only (AF-filtered and
   personalized declined), n = 1 graph per column and EXPLORATORY; the head-to-head is
   not a same-build comparison (decision F, our vg 1.68 against their 1.36), so only each
   tool's delta against its own baseline compares; and the 44 Arm 1 releases are jointly
   correlated, so the per-release bound holds and the joint one does not — which a privacy
   venue will check first.
