## The abstract

Pangenome obfuscation tools protect the donors whose haplotypes build the reference;
none releases a path for a target who is not in the cohort. We present a mechanism that
does, with a computable bound on how well any attacker can tell that release from a
decoy's. A Li-Stephens copying model over the cohort's haplotypes defines a baseline
distribution on cohort-supported paths, which the release exponentially tilts toward
paths agreeing with the target. Both depend on the graph and cohort alone, so the tilt
is the only target-dependent term, calibrated against a utility bounded in [0,1]. We
prove any two input paths induce release distributions at total variation distance at
most `tau`, so no two-way distinguisher exceeds accuracy (1 + `tau`)/2. Because `tau`
saturates at these tilts, we give instead an accuracy ceiling from an exact KL
divergence over all 22 autosomes (22,134,562 positions) against an allele-flipped
decoy — a Pinsker bound, so the true ceiling is lower. Tilting moves that ceiling from
0.500 to 1.000, where it is vacuous, and three implemented distinguishers do not
significantly exceed it. Utility is movement from the untilted, target-free mosaic: a
ceiling of 0.756 recovers a further 1,520 of the target's 4,994,881 non-reference
alleles in expectation. Substituting one obfuscated cohort member into its own 44-member
reference changes a fifth of that member's cells, yet read-mapping rates for five
external donors move at most 0.063 points.

## ⚠ Two errors in my earlier draft of this file, now corrected

Both were caught by a sentence-level audit against the logs and then verified
independently before being applied. Recording them because the first one is the kind
that survives into a submission.

1. **"Five implemented distinguishers stay under the exact ceiling at every point" is
   FALSE.** Two things were wrong. (a) The count: `a1_uniform.md`'s table is headed
   *"Accuracy of the optimal distinguisher"*, singular — D1/D2/D3 are **decoy
   conditions** on one Neyman-Pearson statistic, not three distinguishers. The
   implemented set is three: the optimal distinguisher, rarity-weighted genotype linkage
   and the segment-restricted likelihood ratio. (b) The stay-under claim:
   `a34_uniform.md` **skips** the `acc_ceiling` check entirely and tests only against the
   vacuous `p_succ_bound`. Recomputing its point estimates against the exact chr21
   ceiling puts **ten of them ABOVE it** at the two lowest non-zero `eta`, worst excess
   **+0.0423** (block-512 random-decoy, 0.5650 against a ceiling of 0.522670). Every
   excess is inside the 0.0556 uncorrected minimum detectable advantage and A4's
   max-over-blocks is optimistically biased by construction, so nothing resolves — which
   is why the abstract now says only "do not significantly exceed it". ⚠ The paper must
   state that the formal check for those two attacks was run against the vacuous bound,
   not against the exact ceiling, and that ten raw estimates sit above it within
   resolution. Omitting that is the most refutable thing this paper could do.
2. **"+1,520 non-reference alleles of 2,357,272" put the forbidden level in as a
   denominator.** 2,357,272 is what the *untilted* mosaic already matches — 47.19% of
   `n_nonref` = 4,994,881, carrying zero target information (`docs/terms.md:909`, and the
   run's own JSON prints both side by side). Quoting it as the pool both smuggles in the
   free-lunch level and misstates the share 2.1x. The denominator is 4,994,881;
   +1,519.5 against it is **+0.030%** of the recoverable set, not 0.064%. The abstract
   now gives the count and the true pool and no percentage at all.

A third error entered when I compressed 370 words to 230: *"changes a fifth of its
allele cells"* left the pronoun pointing at the 44-member reference, making the claim
44x too large (0.465%, not 20%) — and in the direction that flatters us, since the
sentence's force comes from the perturbation sounding large. Fixed to "that member's
cells". The 370-word version had it right; the cut broke it.

## What changed from your half-draft

1. **"0/44 at every setting" is not what the table says, and the head-to-head is now out
   of the abstract entirely.** `pm_attacks.md` reads 0/44 at `eta_tau` 0..2821 and
   **1/44 at 5642**; membership inference puts 1/44 at rank 1 at `eta_tau` = 500. More
   importantly the **untilted** release is also 0/44, so what defeats their linkage
   attacks is the release FORM — a cohort mosaic — not the knob. Stating that honestly
   costs ~35 words, which a 220-word abstract cannot carry, so the comparison moves to
   the results where it can be made properly. See the first open choice below.
2. **The two privacy statements are now separate and in order.** The theorem gives
   (1 + `tau`)/2, which is ~1 in the reported regime and carries no information; the
   number we report is the exact ceiling. Your draft merged them.
3. **`acc_ceiling` is not "the total variation".** It is one half of one plus Pinsker's
   upper bound on the TV, from an exact KL. The abstract says "an accuracy ceiling from
   an exact KL divergence ... a Pinsker bound, so the true ceiling is lower" — "lower",
   not "tighter", because "tighter" reads as *our bound is nearly tight*, the opposite of
   what `docs/terms.md` requires us to say.
4. **"Bounded by the accuracy ceiling" inverts what an attack can show.** Standing
   constraint 4: measured accuracy is a LOWER bound on distinguishability. Attacks can
   only fail to reach the ceiling.
5. **Utility is a movement, never a level.** `e_q_u_over_ceiling` = 0.859 is reached at
   `eta_tau` = 0 by a mosaic carrying zero target information.
6. **Dropped the unspent-budget framing entirely**, per your instruction — it is not even
   a clause now. It belongs in the results, next to the attack table where the numbers
   that support it already live.
7. **Dropped the novelty claim about the guarantee.** The supported claim is about the
   SETTING (a target outside the cohort); privvg's exponential mechanism carries a DP
   guarantee, so "first with a guarantee" would be refuted by its own paper. privvg and
   PanMixer are both uncited in the abstract now for length; both must be cited in the
   intro.
8. **Named the arm where the arm changes what is true.** The read-mapping sentence is
   Arm 1 — the target IS a cohort member — which sits against the abstract's own opening
   premise, so it now says "one obfuscated cohort member into its own reference".

## Open choices for you

1. **The head-to-head is out; put it back?** It is the content a reviewer most wants and
   the honest form costs about 35 words: *"PanMixer's own Gap Score links 0 of 44 of our
   genome-wide releases at every setting but the most permissive, against 55 of 100 for
   its own; the untilted release is linked 0 of 44 too, so the credit belongs to the
   released object's mosaic form and not to the tilt."* Adding that means cutting the
   read-mapping sentence (30 words). My read: read mapping is the cleaner buy — it is the
   project's first positive biological-utility result and carries no attribution trap —
   but this is an emphasis call and it is yours.
2. **The attack number is gone from the abstract and I think that is right.** 0.826 is a
   **chr21 chromosome-scope** release (T = 332,521) while the ceiling range quoted is
   genome-wide (T = 22,134,562), and `docs/data.md` says of exactly this pairing: *"the
   same privacy level per release as the genome-wide table, but a DIFFERENT released
   object. Never pool the two."* The grid is matched on `acc_ceiling`, so they can be read
   at one privacy level — but not with the scope dropped, and the stamp costs more words
   than the number is worth here. It belongs in the results with its scope on it.
3. **"worst-case decoy" — still not written.** The allele-flipped decoy is the hardest we
   evaluated; nothing in `docs/` proves it maximises KL over all admissible decoys. The
   abstract says "an allele-flipped decoy", which is what the number supports.
4. **Can we compute `acc_ceiling` for PanMixer's releases?** My reading of their code says
   no, structurally: their knapsack with a fixed seed makes a release a deterministic
   function of (`capacity`, seed), so there is no release *distribution* for a KL between
   two inputs to live on. Re-posing their optimiser as a sampler would be a redesign of
   their mechanism, not an evaluation. ⚠ This is a code reading, not a measurement —
   worth an independent check before it reaches the paper as a claim.
5. **`epsilon_tau` stays out.** At this grid it is 500 to 11,280 per release, which reads
   alarmingly until divided by T. A number needing a paragraph to stop misleading does not
   belong in an abstract.
6. **Caveats that must reach the paper but not the abstract:** read mapping is chr21, one
   target of 44, full-graph construction only, n = 1 graph per column, EXPLORATORY; the
   head-to-head is not same-build (our vg 1.68 against their 1.36), so only each tool's
   delta against its own baseline compares; and the 44 Arm 1 releases are jointly
   correlated, so the per-release bound holds and the joint one does not — the limitation
   a privacy venue checks first.
