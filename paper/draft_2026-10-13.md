## Summary of what was found

The mechanism is correct, its privacy guarantee holds with a very large margin, and at
genome scale it buys almost no utility. Those three statements are the same fact seen from
three sides, and the measured size of the effect is the result of this paper.

Across the entire privacy range — from `tau` = 0, which applies no protection at all, to
`tau` = 0.99 — the expected number of released cells differing from the target moves by
**4.31 cells**, against a level of 7,630,132 differing cells out of **43,958,219 called**
(Arm 1, 44 held-out individuals, genome-wide, exact). As a rate that is a change in
`target_fidelity` of **-9.8015e-08**. ⚠ The denominator is the CALLED cells, not the
differing ones: dividing by 7,630,132 would overstate the reach 5.76x. The
expected utility moves by **+9.6e-08** in Arm 1 and **+8.3e-08** in Arm 2, two independent
measurements on different targets, different panels and different ceilings.

Budget dilution is the proximate explanation and its SIZE is measured, but the mechanism
behind it is **not** established: two first-order predictions of the movement both failed,
in opposite directions (Section 2), so no causal account is claimed here. `tau` buys one
unit of utility budget spread across the whole genome, so with 22,134,562 weighted
positions each position receives `beta_v` ~ 4.5178e-08, so `eta_tau * beta_v` is
1.1957e-07 at `tau` = 0.99 and the REALISED per-haplotype tilt, which the diploid release
applies at half `eta_tau`, is **5.9785e-08**. That is below the resolution at which
the sampler's donor choices can change. The same code on a toy genome, where
`mean beta_v` = 0.111, moves expected utility 0.4502 -> 0.6477.

**This is a statement about the mechanism's reach at genome scale, not a failure to
implement it.** The sampler is exact, proven against enumeration on a cohort small enough
to enumerate, and the privacy bound holds everywhere it was checked.

## Section 1 — Arm 1: the head-to-head

**Construction.** The 44 HPRC cohort members as targets, diploid, 22 autosomes, with
per-target leave-one-out: both of the target's haplotypes leave the donor panel, so
K = 86. 308 releases — 44 individuals x the 7 pre-registered `tau` points — one release per
genome per `tau`. Provenance `logs/B_arm1_results.md`, `logs/B_arm1_release.md`,
`logs/B_arm1_endpoint.json`.

Every record was verified on read: arm, genome scope over all 22 autosomes, `loo_id`
equal to its own target, and `log_z_p` inside `[0, eta_tau]`. 308 of 308 pass.

**The chain is per target, and not a subset of Arm 2's.** Removing two haplotypes lowers
`support(v)` and shrinks every donor intersection, so a zero-genetic-distance block that
some donor spans at K = 88 may be spanned by none at K = 86 — which dead-ends the sampler.
Decision K is therefore re-applied per target. Measured over all 44 x 22 = 968 chains
(`logs/B_loo_chain_survey.md`): `in_chain` T spans 22,129,531 to 22,136,532 against Arm 2's
22,134,562, and the `untraversable_block` count spans 19 to 21 against Arm 2's 20.

⚠ **The exclusion set is NOT monotone in the donor panel.** On chr16 the 2,026-position
block Arm 2 excludes becomes traversable once HG02630 is held out, putting 1,981 positions
in that target's chain that are not in Arm 2's. Dropping positions shortens the surviving
zero-distance runs, and a shorter run needs a donor to span fewer positions. So Arm 1's
chain must never be built by subsetting Arm 2's, which is the obvious implementation.

### The primary endpoint

`E[differ]` from the posterior state marginals, exact, with no sampling and no
differencing. `target_fidelity` is that count over the target's called cells, and it is a
**differ rate: lower is more faithful**.

| metric | median at `tau` = 0 | movement to `tau` = 0.99 |
|---|---|---|
| `target_fidelity` * | 0.173716006 | **-9.8015e-08** |
| `e_differ` | 7,630,132.36 cells | **-4.3056 cells** |
| `e_q_u` | 0.802982751 | **+9.6048e-08** |
| `u_ceiling` | 0.972588907 | — |

n = 44 held-out individuals, K = 86, 22 autosomes, exact. Per-target movement spans
-1.2129e-07 to -9.0451e-08, and **44 of 44 targets move in the faithful direction on all
three quantities**, so the effect is systematic rather than noise.

⚠⚠ **The LEVEL is not attributable to the mechanism.** About 17.4% of called cells are
altered at `tau` = 0, where the release is an untilted draw carrying zero target
information. A cohort member's alleles are largely carried by the other 86 haplotypes, and
the mosaic can only emit alleles some donor actually has. Reporting the level as retained
utility would credit the mechanism with human genetic similarity. **The movement is the
figure.**

⚠ `donor_switches` is essentially flat in `tau` at a median of ~830,714, which the theory
predicts: the tilt cannot create or remove a switch (below), only reassign donors within a
fixed switch skeleton.

### The comparison with PanMixer

Scope chr21, which is where their measured sweep exists and is `capacity`'s native scope.
Both sides are scored by **one function** on each tool's own final released object, not
read from either tool's log. `utility_loss_panmixer` uses their shipped support over all
K = 88 — the target included — on both sides.

✅ **Validated before our numbers were quoted.** Our scoring reproduces their reported
`changed_alleles` and `total_alleles` exactly at all 8 capacities, and their total possible
cost 9288.854446 reproduces their logged 9288.85 from their own array.

| | cost (masked, share of own total) | `target_fidelity` |
|---|---|---|
| PanMixer, capacity 0.01 | 0.009969 | 0.012045 |
| PanMixer, capacity 0.10 | 0.088843 | 0.105043 |
| PanMixer, capacity 0.30 (saturated) | 0.137106 | 0.164090 |
| PanMixer, capacity 0.50-0.90 | 0.137125 | 0.164113 |
| **Ours, all seven `tau`** | **0.145880 (exact)** | **0.167125** |

chr21, HG00438, scoring support K = 88, n = 1 target. Provenance
`logs/B_arm1_headtohead.md`.

**The result: they offer a frontier and we offer a point at its far end.** Their
`target_fidelity` spans 0.012045 to 0.164113 and saturates from capacity 0.3 upward. ⚠ The
optimiser does NOT stop spending there — between capacity 0.3 and 0.5 its own stats record
18,640 more moves and 10.4% more cost (2,786.66 to 3,077.60). What stops changing is the
released OBJECT: 106,534 altered cells against 106,549. We report that as an observation
and attribute no mechanism to their code. Ours sits at 0.167125
and does not move with `tau`. So `capacity -> fidelity` is not invertible above their
saturation and our level sits at or above it: **no matched-fidelity capacity exists**, and
none is reported. Nothing is interpolated.

⚠ Our `tau` cells span 0.162610 to 0.168011 across the grid, but that spread is draw noise
on independent seeds — the exact `tau` effect is -9.8e-08 — so this says our fidelity sits
at their ceiling, not that it is distinguishably worse.

⚠⚠ **The cost ordering reverses with the missing-data convention, so neither figure stands
alone.** Their formula compares raw, without masking missing calls. Under that rule our
cost share is 0.271263 against theirs 0.009973-0.148686; masked, ours is 0.145880 against
theirs 0.009969-0.137125. The measured cause: the convention accounts for **46.2% of our
cost and 0.045%-7.78% of theirs**. Their released object is the original with selected
blocks replaced, so it preserves the original's missing calls; ours is a complete mosaic
and cannot carry a missing allele at all. Every cell where the target is uncalled is
therefore altered in our output by construction and only sometimes in theirs. **The cost
counts uncalled-target cells as alterations on both sides, and that must be stated
wherever the number appears.**

✅ The alignment axis is unaffected: both sides mask missing calls when computing
`target_fidelity`, so the convention difference touches only the cost row.

⚠⚠ **This is not a criticism of PanMixer.** Its goal is donor protection; preserving the
original's missing calls is consistent with that goal, and we claim no defect in its design
or its scoring scope. What differs is what the two mechanisms emit — a path release is
complete by construction — and a metric defined on one does not transfer unchanged to the
other.

⚠ Their reported `utility_loss` is their optimiser's objective rather than a recomputation
on the final stacked object, and the two quantities diverge by 102% at capacity 0.3. **This
is a difference in which quantity is reported, NOT a defect** — the objective is the right
thing for an optimiser to report, and their own saturation is visible in their own numbers.
We recompute both sides so that one definition is applied to both; parsing their log would
have compared our realised cost against their budget.

## Section 2 — Arm 2: the external target at genome scale

**Construction.** The target is outside `G`, outside `D`, outside every panel and outside
the attack database, so the full cohort is the donor panel at K = 88 and nothing is held
out. Nothing in the mechanism is a function of the target except the tilt.

### Privacy

The bound is exact, not sampled. `kl_exact` is computed from the state marginals and
`acc_ceiling` follows by Pinsker.

## Why the two arms agree, and what that means

Arm 1 and Arm 2 measure the same quantity on different people, different panels, different
ceilings and different values of K. Their expected-utility movements are **+9.6048e-08** and
**+8.345e-08**. Budget dilution is therefore a property of the mechanism at genome scale and
not an artefact of one configuration.

⚠⚠ **The privacy ceiling and the utility movement are NOT measured on the same release,
so they are not yet one fact.** At `tau` = 0.99 the Pinsker bound puts total variation at or
below **3.109e-03** against the 0.99 that release is permitted — about 0.3% of the
distinguishability allowed. But that figure is Arm 2 at **chr21 with `beta_share` = 1**,
T = 332,521, K = 88, n = 10 targets, which is 66.6x more generous per position than the
genome-wide release the utility movements above are measured on. Those are different
utility functions on different releases, and constraint 8 does not permit reading them as
one fact. The genome-wide `kl_exact` and `acc_ceiling` are **not measured**.

The qualitative expectation — that a release whose tilt is nearly inert is also nearly
undetectable — is consistent with both measurements and is in the conservative direction,
since the genome-wide per-position tilt is the smaller of the two. **But it is a conjecture
until one release carries both numbers**, and closing that gap is the first thing to run
next.

### The budget can be reallocated, and it is free in privacy terms

This is exploratory and is **not** a pre-registered endpoint; it is reported because a
reader will ask whether the budget could be spent better.

The bound depends on exactly one property of the utility function: its global range
`delta_u = sum_v beta_v`. Hold that at 1 and `tau`, `eta_tau`, `epsilon_tau` and
`p_succ_bound` are identical however `beta_v` is distributed. Measured on chr21 at
`beta_share` = 1 (`logs/B_beta_concentration.md`), the production allocation moves `e_q_u`
by +3.6368e-06 across the grid while **10 contiguous positions move it by +1.6546e-02,
about 4,500x more**, with the privacy parameters unchanged on every row.

⚠ That is not more utility. It is a narrower definition of utility: each allocation is a
different utility function, and a release faithful at ten declared positions is a weaker
claim about a genome. ⚠ And the honest reading is the headroom, not the movement — the
single-position allocation captures 73% of its available headroom, but its headroom is
0.004164, because the baseline already matches that position 99.6% of the time. **The
positions where the tilt has the most power are mostly the ones that needed it least.**

### Why the tilt cannot do more

A choice changes the release only if the utility it offers outruns what it costs under the
baseline:

> `eta_tau / 2` x (the `beta_v` mass that choice controls)  >  the choice's cost in `-log R_D`

Donor identity within a fixed switch skeleton costs **zero** — every off-diagonal transition
entry is the same `s_t / K` — so the tilt is the only decider there, and that is where all
of its effect already lives. Buying or removing a switch is unreachable at any usable
`tau`. Measured directly over the 331,462 chr21 steps that carry non-zero genetic
distance (332,520 steps in all, of which 1,058 have zero distance and forbid a switch
outright), the median diversion cost is **10.1529 nats**, against at most
`eta_tau / 2` = 1.3233 nats on offer at `tau` = 0.99. ⚠ These three figures are a direct
measurement reported in `docs/memory.md`; they are quoted in `logs/B_arm1_results.md` but
were not computed by the script that emitted it. Breaking even on one
median switch would need a `beta_v` mass of 7.67 at `tau` = 0.99, and the entire budget is
1. **The tilt can never create or remove a donor switch.**

## Limitations, stated rather than discovered by a reader

1. ⚠⚠ **The head-to-head is not a same-build comparison.** PanMixer's retained sweep ran on
   its own vg-1.36-derived inputs. Nothing of its mechanism has run on our vg-1.68 build, so
   "both arms on one consistent build" holds on our side only. Every cross-tool cell is
   cross-build, which is why only shares of each side's own total are quoted and never raw
   counts.
2. ⚠⚠ **Arm 1's guarantee is conditional on the graph.** `G` was built from all HPRC
   assemblies including each target's, so even a full leave-one-out only approximates the
   external-target threat model. Leave-one-out removes the target from the donor panel; it
   cannot remove it from the graph topology.
3. ⚠⚠ **The joint bound over Arm 1's 44 releases does not hold.** Their donor panels are
   each other's true haplotypes. The per-release bound holds; the joint one does not.
4. ⚠ **The `tau` grid is a declared evaluation device, never a release procedure.** Seven
   releases of one genome compose. A deployment makes one.
5. ⚠ **The 44 targets carry 44 different utility functions.** Leave-one-out gives each its
   own chain, its own `T`, its own 22 `beta_share` values and its own ceiling, so utilities
   are never pooled and every utility figure is a median over distinct quantities. Pool
   measured attack outcomes, never utilities.
6. ⚠ **The two arms' ceilings are not comparable.** Arm 1's `u_ceiling` of 0.9726 exceeds
   Arm 2's 0.8257 because a cohort member is called at nearly every position of a graph
   built from its own assembly. That is not a better result.
7. ⚠ **`missing_policy` = RENORMALISE penalises poorly-assembled donors** for a defect that
   is not theirs. Accepted deliberately rather than modelled around at this stage.
8. ⚠ **`capacity` and `tau` are not comparable parameters**, and neither are `eps_pmi` and
   `tau`. `tau` is a privacy parameter with a proof behind it; `capacity` is a utility-loss
   budget and bounds nothing about distinguishability.
9. ⚠ Arm 1's `target_fidelity` ceiling on the leave-one-out panel is **unmeasured**, so no
   figure is quoted against it.
10. ⚠ Not delivered: genome-scope `utility_loss_panmixer`, exact chr21-restricted
    `target_fidelity` per target, and `attacker_accuracy` for Arm 1.
11. ⚠⚠ **THERE IS NO GENOME-WIDE PRIVACY MEASUREMENT, AND IT IS NOT COMPUTABLE BY THIS
    ROUTE.** Every privacy figure above is chr21 at `beta_share` = 1. We attempted the
    genome-wide computation on 2026-10-13 (`scripts/exact_kl_genome.py`, summing the
    per-chromosome KL at each chromosome's share, which is valid because KL is additive
    across chromosomes) and it **failed its own sign check**: `KL` came back at
    **-1.96e-11** on chr19 at `tau` = 0.05, because
    `eta_tau * (E[u_p] - E[u_q])` = 6.768228233e-04 and `log Z_p - log Z_q` =
    6.768228429e-04 agree to ten significant figures. The genome-wide KL is of order
    **3.4e-11** by extrapolation from the chr21 figure, and the cancellation noise of a
    difference between two accumulations over 22,134,562 positions is of order **2e-11**.
    **The signal is at the noise floor of the estimator.** This is the same numerical
    failure mode already recorded for the finite-difference derivative route at genome
    scale, and the sign check — `KL >= 0` is a theorem, not a convention — is what caught
    it. ⚠ The DIRECTION is known: the genome-wide per-position tilt is 66.6x smaller, so
    the genome-wide ceiling is closer to 0.5 than the chr21 one, i.e. more private. The
    MAGNITUDE is unmeasured, and a route that does not difference two nearly-equal
    accumulations would be needed to measure it.

## Required before submission

- ⚠⚠ **The proposal's introduction defines `tau` incorrectly** and needs a one-line fix.
  It reads "distinguished with the probability of no more than `tau`", which is impossible
  for `tau` < 0.5 since a coin achieves 0.5. The missing word is ADVANTAGE:
  advantage = 2(accuracy - 1/2) = TV, so `tau` bounds the attacker's edge over a coin, not
  its success rate. The abstract states it correctly.
- Decide whether to run PanMixer's preprocessing and obfuscator on our build, which would
  make the head-to-head same-build. HG00438 is both their swept subject and one of our 44,
  so a same-build, same-target frontier is available in principle.
- Decide whether to pre-register a public-panel allocation as a secondary endpoint, which
  is what the reallocation finding above would become if adopted.
